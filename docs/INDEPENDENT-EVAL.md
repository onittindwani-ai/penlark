# Independent evaluation (held out sets A and B)

Two test sets, A and B, were written by separate AI test authors who never saw the code, from three documents only (the spec, the start guide and the capabilities page). Each set has 4 sample authors with 20 training samples each, 60 held out texts (10 per author, 10 by an outsider, 10 generic machine sounding texts), 51 drafts with planted house rule violations and clean ones with keys, and 20 notes with 5 planted themes and 4 decoys. The scorer is `tools/eval_independent.py`; its readings were fixed in its docstring before any run. The files are in `data/eval/independent/`. Everything is sample data. Scores come from plain code, not a language model.

## What was run, in this order
1. Set A, run once with the engine as it was after the first build: `set_a_first_run.json`, saved untouched.
2. General improvements (list below), made from general language knowledge, never from any item of A or B.
3. Set B, run once after those improvements: `set_b_first_run.json`, saved untouched.
4. Set A again with the final engine: `set_a_after_improvements.json`. **Set A is no longer held out after step 2**: it was run once before and its aggregate numbers were read, so treat the second column as a re run, not a test.

Before step 1 only the authors' README was read. After the runs only printed aggregates and counts by flag kind were read; no text, draft or note of either set was opened. The final engine (after the model path was added) was run on both sets again and gave identical results.

The older self written held out numbers, for comparison: 24 of 24 right author, mean AUC 0.975, outsider mean best 56.7, generic text mean best 79.0 (7 of 8 closest to the formal profile), house rule precision and recall 1.0, 2 of 3 idea themes. The independent numbers are lower or more mixed on the points that matter, which is the reason the sets exist.

"Full gate" is what the product does before a draft can go to approval: voice match at least 60 AND no house rule flag AND no claim without a source AND no [BRACKET] left.

## Plain reading
- **Telling the authors apart works.** About 93 to 95 percent of author texts go to the right profile, and the look alike pairs (A1 and A3, A2 and A4, B1 and B2, B3 and B4) were separated in 78 of 80 author texts. One terse profile (A2) pulls in a few texts of its look alike.
- **A generic machine sounding text does NOT reliably score below 60 on voice alone.** On both sets the generic texts scored a mean best of about 61, and 4 to 5 of 10 passed 60 on some profile (the terse and casual profiles are the usual home). The self written set gave 79, so that picture was too bleak, but this is still not a safe gate. What stops them is the house rules: all 20 generic texts hit the banned phrase list or the reading level limit, so none reached approval (full gate 0 of 20). The voice match is a style measure; the banned list and the reading level do the work against machine prose.
- **An outsider can look like a casual author.** Set A's outsider (a dry pharmacist) scored a mean best of 56 and never passed. Set B's outsider (a punchy sports columnist, chatty like two of the authors) scored a mean best of 74.7 and all 10 texts passed the full gate. The voice match cannot tell a different person who writes in a similar register. Do not read a high match as proof of authorship. A threshold sweep (information only, never used to choose anything) shows a minimum of 80 would have stopped set B's outsider and still passed 29 of 40 real texts. The owner decided 60 as the default; an implementer can raise it for a client.
- **The house rules found the banned phrases and dashes, and miss claims.** Banned phrase recall was 8 of 12 on the first look at set A (4 missed) and 12 of 12 after the general changes, on both sets; set B was only run after those changes, so it does not show how many the old rules would have missed there. Dash recall was 8 of 8 and reading level 5 of 5 on every run. Unsourced claim recall is the weak spot: 6 of 10 on A first, 8 of 10 on A after, 4 of 10 on B (6 missed). Reading level raised 2 false flags on B.
- **Real writing trips the full gate.** Only 20 to 27 of 40 genuine author texts pass the full gate. These held out texts are personal stories, which the claims check treats as claims on purpose, and one author's plain trade talk hit entries of the built in banned list. That is the strict default doing what it was told, but an implementer should expect to turn phrases off or add some per client.
- **The idea finder is weak.** 2 of 5 themes on both sets, decoys avoided 4 of 4 and 3 of 4, quotes always verbatim (they are slices of the note, so that number says little). The finder needs the same words in different notes; a paraphrase across notes is missed.

## General improvements made after the first set A run
Planned in the system's decision notes before coding and judged only on the older self written sets and the seed: banned phrases match with a space or hyphen, curly apostrophes and regular word endings, with <!--n:seed.banned_phrases-->130<!--/n--> built in phrases (was 96); the claims check no longer treats a generic "a patient" as a client claim, counts number words next to people, money and a share, and knows more experience verbs; a better stemmer and 17 small families of everyday words for the idea finder. The voice score curve, the tolerances and the threshold 60 were not touched.
